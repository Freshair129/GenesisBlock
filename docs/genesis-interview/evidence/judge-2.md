Under a cost-realism + requirement-coverage lens on a solo-maintained engine with a committed SQLite pivot, Design C wins: it is the only design whose critical path answers the G3 moat/mirage question fast (moat-bench is a co-equal deliverable, reachable in ~3-4 weeks: Stage 0 baseline + minimal pipeline executor + harness), its 6-8 week estimate is the only one that survives scrutiny, and its tarpit/footgun posture is the strongest (one fixed 4-slot pipeline struct, parse-error-past-the-line, epistemic signal cut with grounded reasoning). Design A is technically the most accurate (its description of hnsw_rs search_filter semantics is the one the crate source actually supports) but builds a second native filter-index engine that fights the committed S2 substrate direction, and its Regime-A latency claim ignores cold-sidecar positioned-read cost. # REFUTATIONS
- [B] CLAIM: "filtered candidates are rejected DURING graph descent, not post-truncated; recall loss of post-filter disappears"
  REFUTED: The pinned crate source (hnsw_rs-0.3.4/src/hnsw.rs, search_layer ~900-1040) shows descent explores ALL neighbours — candidate_points.push happens regardless of filter; only return_points is gated by hnsw_filter. So the classic top-K truncation loss is removed, but the visited/ef budget is spent on filtered-out points: at low selectivity latency balloons and result sets can under-fill. Design A's characterization ('filter applied at result-collection while navigation traverses the full graph') is the one the code supports; B's 'disappears' oversells the mechanism it shares with A.
- [B] CLAIM: node_versions "populated by WAL replay (the projection retains what RAM discards; WAL remains authority per SPEC--SQLITE-SUBSTRATE §3.1)" enabling VERSIONS OF and node SYSTEM-axis time-travel
  REFUTED: WAL compaction is already shipped and committed (PR #28, compact-to-live-state in save_state, built precisely to bound disk ~20GB@1M). SPEC--SQLITE-SUBSTRATE-S0-S1.md requires the projection be 'deleted or corrupted and ... rebuil[t] from authoritative state' (lines 54, 105, 127). A history table cannot be rebuilt from a compacted WAL — historical versions are gone. So node_versions either silently promotes SQLite to a second durability authority (violating the substrate's own §3.1) or forces a rework/disable of WAL compaction. Neither is mentioned or costed anywhere in B; its flagship differentiator is un-implementable as specced.
- [B] CLAIM: "all genuinely hard planning ... is delegated to SQLite's own query planner, which the mission explicitly permits ('no planner beyond SQLite's own')"
  REFUTED: The mission guardrail reads 'No planner tar-pit — pipeline DSL with fixed operator shapes only.' The quoted phrase 'no planner beyond SQLite's own' appears nowhere in the mission contract — B quotes a permit that does not exist. The delegation may still be defensible engineering, but presenting it as an explicit mission license is fabricated grounding.
- [B] CLAIM: "Total: ~9-12 weeks single-maintainer, of which ~5-6 weeks is HQL-attributable"
  REFUTED: Self-serving accounting: G1 filtered ANN (B3) consumes the bitmap produced by B2's node_props EAV, and B2 requires S0 (C-3, HIGH risk, 2-3 weeks of crash-window proofs). The 'not new cost attributable to HQL' framing hides that every G1 and G3 measurement sits behind the substrate critical path — the honest time-to-first-number is the full 9-12 weeks, directly conflicting with guardrail 3 (measure, don't assert) since B is the slowest design to produce any falsifying benchmark.
- [A] CLAIM: "~4096 scalar-L2 evals at dim 1024 ≈ low-single-digit ms worst case, and at GKS-realistic selectivity (hundreds of atoms) ≈ tens of µs with PERFECT recall"
  REFUTED: Regime A scores bitmap rows through 'the existing sidecar/arena rerank path' — and the sidecar is OFF-RAM by design (SidecarReader positioned reads, LRU capped at 4096 rows ≈16MiB, lib.rs:1034-1058). Cold-cache worst case is thousands of positioned reads on a SATA SSD (~0.1ms class each), i.e. potentially hundreds of ms, not 'low-single-digit ms'. The claim holds only for warm LRU hits or arena-resident f32 collections; the design never states that qualifier, and its own bench discipline (warm/cold, p50+p99) would expose it.
- [A] CLAIM: Example E2: MATCH (a {id:"g0"})-[:LINK*1..6]->(b) FANOUT 100000 RETURN b.id LIMIT 1000 ("FANOUT here = frontier row cap")
  REFUTED: Not derivable from A's own grammar: fanout_spec is attached only to the `expand` rule, and match_pattern is outside the `pipeline` production entirely (query = pipeline | traverse | match_pattern | context). E2 silently overloads FANOUT with a second meaning (frontier row cap vs per-node breadth cap) on a command that grammatically cannot carry it — a small but telling sign of surface creep in a design whose tarpit defense rests on 'unparseable, not badly-planned'.
- [C] CLAIM: "real filter-aware HNSW (ACORN-class, inside hnsw_rs or a fork) is required — that exceeds minimal-delta scope" (and lists filter-aware HNSW under deliberately-not-built tar-pits)
  REFUTED: False dichotomy that mis-prices C's cheapest G1 upgrade: the pinned hnsw_rs 0.3.4 already ships FilterT + search_filter (verified: filter.rs:7-19 blanket-impls closures, hnsw.rs:1475), wireable at the single existing seam VecIndex::search_f32 with a roaring-bitmap closure — roughly a week of work per both competing designs, no fork, no research. C's post-filter+adaptive-refetch (with worst-case double HNSW work, its own risk 8) is chosen against a strictly better option the grounding hands it for free; this is the main reason its G1 score is lowest.
- [C] CLAIM: Stage 1 grammar is "~600-900 lines ... zero engine risk", including `tier = { "H0".."H6" }`
  REFUTED: That production is not valid pest — the `..` range operator applies to single characters ('0'..'9'), not string literals, so the grammar as sketched fails to compile. Same stage: the pipeline rule makes trav_stage mandatory, so a vector-seed + RANK BY query with no hop (the gks_recall shape without a graph leg) is unparseable. Both are trivial fixes, but they show the 'zero engine risk' Stage 1 estimate rests on unchecked grammar sketches, and the fuzz-corpus extension cost for ~10 new keywords is real, not epsilon.
