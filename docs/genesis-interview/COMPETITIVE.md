# COMPETITIVE — Genesis · Who We Actually Fight in the Buyer Tiers

> Scope: the two buyer tiers from ROUND3 — **Tier B** (memory substrate for a local multi-agent orchestrator) and **Tier C** (on-device flagship agent: local hot store + privacy-curated CRDT sync). Tier A (bare SLM worker) is served by three simple tools and is not a purchase decision — its "competitor" is SQLite+sqlite-vec, settled in ROUND2.
> Evidence: fresh 2026-07-06 web research in `evidence/r4-frameworks.md`, `r4-ondevice.md`, `r4-rivals.md` (all URLs there); engine-side facts from Rounds 1–3. Both buyer tiers are still roleplay-derived hypotheses (ROUND3) — this maps the field *if* they hold.

## 1. The frame: the enemy is different in each tier

**Tier B's enemy is not a database.** It is the *path of zero resistance*: the framework's built-in checkpointer plus Redis or SQLite. Every orchestration framework ships persistence adapters; Redis ships a first-party LangGraph checkpointer, a cross-thread vector store, and now a dedicated **Agent Memory Server** (MCP-exposed, LLM fact extraction) — Redis is explicitly colonizing agent memory from the server end. The DIY stack's real gaps match our differentiators exactly — no fleet-wide point-in-time state (LangGraph "time travel" is per-thread replay of opaque blobs), no cross-agent graph queries, no engine-enforced audit, no DB-side fusion — but those needs are *latent*: builders discover them after their first serious multi-agent debugging incident, not before (r4-rivals §1, §"who would a builder pick").

**Tier C's enemy is a partitioned field with a hole in the middle.** CRDT-without-vector (Ditto), vector-without-CRDT (ObjectBox, Couchbase Lite, libSQL/Turso), sync-plumbing-without-a-database (PowerSync, ElectricSQL). **Graph and temporal are effectively zero across all ten players surveyed** — no one exposes a time-travel/point-in-time query surface (SwiftData's limited history fetch is the lone partial exception) — and device-controlled partial sync is nearly vacant: every shipping partial-sync mechanism (PowerSync sync rules, Electric shapes, Couchbase channels, ObjectBox user-sync) is *server*-defined; only Ditto's subscriptions are device-declared, and those govern what *arrives* — its outbound curation is coarse per docs, granularity unconfirmed (r4-ondevice, axis table + bottom line; Couchbase's device-side push filters are deprecated-status-unverified). The market event: **Realm/Atlas Device Sync hit EOL 2025-09-30** — a vendor-burned installed base is migrating now and is allergic to closed commercial sync.

Key structural facts (r4-rivals §5, r4-frameworks cross-cutting): **nobody in the 2025–26 agent-memory wave ships a new engine** — all bottom out in Redis/Neo4j/Postgres/SQLite/Kuzu; **nobody combines run-level replay + bitemporal shared graph + audit in one queryable store**; and the checkpointer pattern has demonstrated production fragility (Check Point's 2026 SQLi-to-RCE in LangGraph's checkpointer). Graphiti's LongMemEval lead over Mem0 on temporal retrieval (63.8% vs 49.0%, particula.tech) shows the *temporal* axis wins evaluations — and Graphiti does it at the app layer over a server DB, which is exactly the slot an embedded bitemporal engine fills with less machinery.

**Our honest position in tier B:** the uncontested combination (engine-enforced bitemporal + graph + vector, embedded, ROUND2) is real, and the fleet needs map to shipped-but-unexposed machinery (`events_since`, signed events — ROUND3). What we must not claim: snapshot isolation (conceded, G-R3.2) or proven demand (OPEN: Rwang/GoVibe trace).

**Our honest position in tier C:** on paper we hold the two empty columns (graph, bitemporal) plus on-device vector — CRDT is *not* an empty column (Ditto owns it; cr-sqlite proved it); our sync differentiator is *what* syncs, a bitemporal graph+vector store, not having CRDT. In practice (G-R3.4): **our sync is a unit-tested prototype** — no transport, zero FFI sync symbols, no curated-export API, no conflict journal, compaction×delta untested, iOS stub. The "privacy-curated export" axis is genuinely vacant across the entire field — but it is vacant *for us too* until it is a shipping primitive. Competing here today against Couchbase/Ditto/Turso on sync maturity would be dishonest; the play is the M-class program first (ROUND3), and the Realm-refugee window is time-limited.

## 4. Threats ranked, with watch triggers

1. **T1 — Redis colonizes tier B from above** (Agent Memory Server + first-party checkpointers). Counter-signal to weigh: AMS shows low community pull so far (~288 stars, r4-frameworks) — corporate push, not organic demand yet. Trigger to react: Redis AMS adds any point-in-time/versioned-state feature.
2. **T2 — the Graphiti #1240 slot closes without us.** FalkorDB Lite lands as Graphiti's embedded backend → the leading temporal-memory framework gets a zero-config local story, and our cleanest channel evaporates. Trigger: #1240 merges a FalkorDB-Lite driver. Counter-move: propose a GenesisBlockDB Graphiti driver *now* — Graphiti's driver architecture is a well-defined surface (the *thin-subclass* ease cited in #1240 is FalkorDB-Lite-specific; ours is a fresh driver implementation).
3. **T3 — Turso ships stable offline sync + DiskANN in the Rust rewrite** and owns "local agent database" mindshare with MIT license + SQLite brand gravity. Trigger: rewrite sync GA or vector completion (their issue #832).
4. **T4 — SurrealDB hardens embedded mode** and markets KV time-travel as "agent memory versioning". Trigger: SurrealKV becomes the production default in 3.x.
5. **T5 — Mem0/Letta internalize their stores** (Mem0 v3 already replaced external graph DBs with built-in entity linking) → the framework layer stops needing any engine. Trigger: Letta's git-based memory or Mem0's internal graph gains real query/versioning depth.
6. **T6 — LadybugDB ships a temporal extension.** Three releases show no movement, but it is one extension from axis parity. Trigger: any temporal/versioning item in their release notes.
7. **T7 — a platform vendor ships an on-device agent-memory API.** Low probability per cycle, fatal for tier C if it lands.

## 5. Where the wedges are (and what not to fight)

- **Tier B wedge:** the post-incident buyer — "reconstruct what the fleet's shared state was when run #412 failed" is unanswerable on Redis+SQLite+LangGraph today, and it becomes one `AS OF` query here **once the G-R3.3 audit items land** (edge `superseded_by` wiring, `node_versions` projection) — today the engine answers it for edges only, and selling more than that repeats the mistake Rounds 2–3 exist to prevent. Sell the audit/debug story, not "faster than Redis". **Do not fight**: Redis on raw KV/vector speed at server scale, or LangGraph on orchestration UX — ride its checkpointer/BaseStore interfaces instead (both are public extension points, and a store that makes LangGraph's replay *queryable across threads* upgrades rather than replaces it).
- **Tier C wedge:** OSS, engine-enforced bitemporal + graph + vector in-process, with *device-side* curated export as the headline primitive nobody ships — aimed at the Realm-refugee/local-first cohort that is now allergic to closed sync (Couchbase EE-paywalls vector; ObjectBox core is closed; Ditto is fully commercial). **Do not fight**: Ditto on mesh/P2P transport, PowerSync/Electric on Postgres-org plumbing, or anyone on sync maturity until the G-R3.4 program ships.
- **Channel-first posture:** the entire memory-framework wave needs a store and ships none. Two named, specified integration surfaces exist today: a **Graphiti driver** (#1240) and a **LangGraph checkpointer/BaseStore**. Both look like days-to-weeks, not months `⚠ estimate, un-scoped`, and both put the engine under someone else's growth curve — the substrate position the MSP pivot already chose.

## Verdict
In tier B the fight is against *defaults* (LangGraph+Redis/SQLite) and *narrative* (Graphiti owns "temporal agent memory"), not against any engine — the engine slot is empty, SurrealDB is the only rival with shipped time-travel and it lacks valid-time semantics; in tier C the field is partitioned (CRDT xor vector xor plumbing), graph+temporal are at zero everywhere, and the Realm EOL opened a time-limited OSS window — but our sync is a prototype, so the tier-C wedge is earned only after the M-class sync program ships. The single highest-leverage competitive move this quarter: **claim the Graphiti #1240 embedded-backend slot before FalkorDB Lite does** — it is the cheapest act that simultaneously blocks T2, validates tier-B demand with a real integration, and puts our bitemporal engine under the framework that leads Mem0 on LongMemEval temporal retrieval (one third-party test, particula.tech).
