3. The two moat axes

Bitemporal data model

Every node/edge carries valid_from/valid_to; evolution is supersession (supersede_node), not destructive overwrite. retract_edge is a bitemporal soft-delete. Verified absent in all six same-tier engines measured here and in the enterprise field (Qdrant, Weaviate, Milvus, Neo4j, SurrealDB, pgvector, Elastic — all checked 2026-07-03).

Signed, verifiable audit trail

Events are ed25519-signed; governance tiers enforce MASTER-tier lockout in the engine, not the transport layer; consensus votes are signature-checked with a Merkle root. No enterprise or same-tier vendor ships this natively — including SurrealDB 3.0's SurrealDS, which is quorum-consensus, not CRDT, and has no signed audit trail.

These are the only two axes simultaneously ahead of the entire same-tier and enterprise field, and the only claims in this document with zero recall-condition or scale-condition asterisk. See ADR §1.1 and §4 for the external verification pass.

6. Memory & quantization detail (RSS × quant × scale)

Whole-process RSS, not just the vector arena — this is why the "4×/32×" per-vector quantization ratio does not translate 1:1 into process-level RAM savings. A ~1.8 GB non-vector floor (HNSW graph links, node/edge metadata, interning maps) is untouched by vector quantization and dominates once vectors shrink.

On-disk, the per-vector ratio is exact for the arena file alone: vec_<name>.bin shrinks 409.6→102.4 MB (4.0×, SQ8) → 12.8 MB (32×, BQ) at 100k — matching the ADR's quantization design exactly. Rerank costs RAM and disk (a full f32 sidecar sits on top, positioned-read off-RAM per ONDISK-RERANK-RSS) — it is recall insurance paid in resources, not free.

Known ops gap surfaced by this audit (not a competitor comparison): the WAL is quant-independent and was not compacted in this measurement path — ~20.4 GB at 1M, 5× the f32 snapshot. Flagged as a production durability concern in P33 §6; WAL compaction has since shipped (see project memory / PR history) but the 1M RSS numbers above predate that fix and are RSS-only, not disk-ceiling claims.

8. What we haven't measured yet

  • HelixDB v3.0.7 (YC, Apache-2.0, Rust) — the closest live same-tier rival. No direct head-to-head exists; their vendor bench (12–16× Neo4j) uses different hardware/topology and cannot be compared to anything on this page.
  • LadybugDB 0.18.0 — this page's LadybugDB numbers are v0.15.3 (3 releases stale as of 2026-07-03); v0.18 added WAL group-commits + ART indexes.
  • Post-P0 empirical RSS sweep @500k/1M for the on-disk rerank sidecar — the 4.00×/32.0× ratios in §6 are a structural proof + green correctness tests, not yet an empirical whole-process measurement at that scale (command ready, not yet run).
  • Competitor ingest/RAM ratios re-run against our current baseline; any competitor at 1M scale; p99 latency anywhere; concurrent mixed read/write QPS; crash-recovery time; REST-layer (Axum) throughput — all P7–P33 harnesses hit the storage core directly, never the HTTP surface.

9. Methodology & conditions glossary

  • Embedded vs server: GenesisBlockDB, Kuzu, LadybugDB, DuckDB, RocksDB, Chroma, and LanceDB all run in-process. Qdrant and Neo4j are client-server — their numbers include a network/protocol round-trip that an embedded engine does not pay. This is called out per-row above; it is a real deployment-shape difference, not a benchmarking trick in either direction.
  • Durability asymmetry: GenesisBlockDB's ingest numbers are durable (WAL fsync per batch). Chroma is in-memory/ephemeral. DuckDB/Kuzu/RocksDB/LadybugDB bulk loads in these audits are non-durable bulk paths (comparable on time-to-queryable, not on durability guarantees).
  • Payload asymmetry: our neighbors() returns full NeighborOutput{node,path} objects; several competitors return bare node ids. Flagged with † wherever it materially affects the number (P29 RocksDB deep-hop rows). Where we still win despite doing more work per result (e.g. vs LadybugDB), that is noted explicitly as a stronger result, not hidden.
  • Recall is a knob, not a fixed ceiling: HNSW's ef_search trades recall for latency. Never compare a bare latency number across engines without also stating the recall at which it was measured — every vector row on this page carries its recall condition for that reason.
  • Scale conditions: "22 µs across 10k→1M" describes our own traversal curve; ratios against named competitors were measured only at 100k/800k unless a row explicitly says otherwise (e.g. Neo4j's 10k row).
  • Dates matter: competitor-side benchmarks are 2026-06-21. Our own numbers were re-verified 2026-06-22 (P31, post-MARK-XIII) and 2026-06-23 (P32/P33). LadybugDB and Kuzu's ecosystem status (archived / superseded) was verified 2026-07-03.

Kuzu note: Apple acqui-hired the Kuzu Inc. team (~2025-10-09; disclosed via EU merger filing 2026-02-11); kuzudb/kuzu was archived read-only 2025-10-10. LadybugDB is the community-maintained MIT fork that continues from Kuzu's last release and is now the comparator an agent-memory builder would actually pick.
